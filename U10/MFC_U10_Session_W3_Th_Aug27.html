White · Coach David · Station C — Defending + Shielding

Two roles, same 1v1 channel. First 5 min: attacker focuses on shielding — body between ball and defender, low hips, legal forearm out (elbow in, not a push), hold possession under pressure. Last 5 min: roles swap and focus shifts to defending body shape — sideways-on, low, patient. New: feel the contact legally — this is the first physical station of the season. No tackling yet. Exp: defender actively applies pressure; shielder must hold for 5 full seconds before turning out.

Defending Concepts — Station C · Coach David Demo (2 min before first group)
  • Body shape — sideways-on, not square: one foot forward, weight on the back foot. Standing square means slow first step in any direction. Sideways means you're already half-turned to recover.
  • Low: bend the knees, stay low. Standing tall = slow feet. Cue: "If your knees aren't bent, you can't move."
  • Distance — an arm's length away: close enough to pressure, far enough not to get beaten in one touch. Too close = one step and they're past you. Too far = they have all day to pick a pass.
  • Slow (be patient): don't dive in. Wait. Every time you lunge, you're gambled and can be beaten. Make them make the first move.
  • Fast (when to step in): heavy touch, head goes down, or they stop — that's the moment. Step in fast and win it.
  • Side (show outside): angle your body to steer them toward the touchline, away from goal. Make the dangerous option hard, the safe option easy.

💡
Coaching tip: Getting comfortable with physical contact is a skill, not a personality trait. Some players will be nervous about it — frame every contact rep as technique. "That's your body doing the work, not your arms." When you see a player hold a good shield for 5 seconds, stop play briefly: "Did you see how still he stayed? That's strength." For the lunge-happy defender: let them get beaten first, then ask what happened. One discovery beats five corrections.
45 – 60
Whiteboard + Water: Positional Zones 15 min

Players know the position names from Session 2 (Aug 15). Today goes deeper — where does each position actually live on the pitch, and why? Use the build-out line as the anchor. Draw it on the board, then walk out onto the pitch and show it in real space.

Three Zones — Build-Out Line as the Anchor (7 min)

Draw the pitch with both build-out lines. Divide into three zones. Ask: "If the striker runs all the way back to our goal, what problem does that create?" Let them answer.

  • Defending Third (goal → our build-out line): GK and 2 defenders own this zone. One defender can push up the touchline to offer the winger a backpass — the other stays back to cover. Never both forward at the same time.
  • Middle Third (between both build-out lines): CM (The Glue) stays central. Wingers stay wide in this zone. When defending, wingers track back to our build-out line. When attacking, they drive forward and deliver crosses from the attacking third.
  • Attacking Third (their build-out line → their goal): Striker's zone. Stay central, stay ready for crosses. When we don't have the ball, drop back to the halfway line so we can build out through the CM.
On-Pitch Walkthrough (8 min)

Take all 18 onto the pitch. Point to the build-out lines — they can see them. Place 7 players in the 2-3-1 and walk around them while explaining each zone. Then ask: "Which zone is the winger in when we're defending?" "Where does the striker go when we don't have the ball?" Quick questions, quick answers, then send them to the SSG.

U10 Pitch — Positional Zones (2-3-1)
💡
Coaching tip: The build-out line is already visible on their pitch — point to it rather than just drawing it. "Defenders live between our goal and that dashed line. CM between the two lines. Striker past the far line." Then watch the SSG: when a player naturally holds their zone without being told, name it immediately. That transfer from whiteboard to game is the whole point.
60 – 80
Small-Sided Games: 3v3 with Full Restarts 20 min

First proper small-sided game with all restarts enforced. Players learned throw-ins in Session 3 and positional zones today — the SSG is where both transfer. Watch for players holding their zone naturally and calling restarts correctly. Coaches pause 5 seconds max for any restart question, then restart immediately.

  • Format: Groups of 6 play 3v3 in their own zone — 3 zones running simultaneously. Each coach referees their zone.
  • All restarts enforced: throw-ins (both hands, both feet, behind head), corner kicks, goal kicks, free kicks for fouls
  • Celebrate when a player correctly identifies the restart before the coach calls it
  • No offside in 3v3 — too small a space. Save it for next time.
  • Short rounds of 4–5 min with 30-second water sips between. Rotate opponents each round so all 6 play together.
  • Bonus point: goal from open play where the scorer stayed in their correct positional zone throughout the build-up
3v3 · Three Zones · 20×15m each
💡
Coaching tip: When a restart situation comes up, pause play for 5 seconds max: "Corner or goal kick — who knows?" Let a player call it. If they're right, say so and restart immediately. If wrong, correct it in one sentence and restart. Never stop play for more than 20 seconds — the game is the teacher. Watch for players spontaneously calling "throw-in!" or "our corner!" — that's the whiteboard working.
80 – 90
Knockout 10 min
Closing Ritual — same every session

All 18. Penalty-style — one shot, miss = out. GK rotates every round. Eliminated players cheer loudly for remaining shooters. New players from the closer mark if needed — never announce it. Run all the way to a winner. End the week on a high.

💡
Coaching tip: After the winner is crowned, gather all 18 for 60 seconds. One question: "What's one thing you learned this week?" Take two answers — one from a new player, one from an experienced one. Affirm both. Then send them off. This 60-second close bookends the week and makes the learning feel intentional.
