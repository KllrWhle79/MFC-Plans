Whiteboard + Water Break: Positions & the 2-3-1 20 min
Session 2 — Positions & the 2-3-1

Move to shaded area. Players sit, water in hand. Draw the pitch and label the key areas. Keep it to 3–4 concepts maximum. Ask questions — do not lecture.

  • Ball over touchline → throw-in for the other team
  • Ball over goal line off a defender → corner kick for the attacking team
  • Ball over goal line off an attacker → goal kick for the defending team
  • Hands: only the goalkeeper, only inside the penalty area
2-3-1 Formation — Positions on the U10 Pitch
💡
Coaching tip: Ask questions, don't lecture. "Who knows what a throw-in is?" Let experienced players answer — it reinforces their knowledge and removes pressure from new ones. "Good thinking — close! Let me show you." Never make a player feel embarrassed for not knowing.
40 – 60
Technical Stations: Ball Comfort Circuit — First Introduction 20 min · 6–7 min per station

First technical block of the season. Three stations — same drills that will run every session through Block 1, with scaling that steps up each week. Today: no pressure, no defenders, coach demonstrates every drill before players begin. The goal is simply to understand what each drill is asking. Groups rotate on the whistle every 6–7 minutes.

Gold · Coach Pete · Station A — Ball Mastery

Dribble freely in a small zone, then on the whistle: body over the ball, sole of the foot on top, stop it completely. Coach demonstrates once — a full walk-through — before players begin. New players: walk pace, any foot. Exp: jog pace, keep the ball moving between whistles using sole rolls and inside-outside taps.

Blue · Coach Brent · Station B — First Touch

Paired drill: partner rolls the ball slowly to feet. Receiver stops it cleanly with the inside of their foot, then rolls it back. Coach demonstrates with a player before pairs begin. New players: focus only on stopping the ball cleanly — no rush. Exp: receive and immediately turn away from partner in 2 touches.

White · Coach David · Station C — Dribble Slalom

Dribble through 6 cones in a line. Coach dribbles through first so players see the full path. New players: walk pace, any foot, coach alongside on the first rep. Exp: jog pace, weak foot going through the cones, dominant foot returning.

ROTATION
Whistle every 6–7 minutes. Coach stays at their station; players come to them. No lines, no waiting — all three stations run simultaneously. These same drills return every session: today is about understanding them, not performing them.

New Players — first time seeing these drills
  • Walk pace — no time pressure on any station
  • Coach demonstrates before every first rep
  • Goal: understand what the drill is asking, not to do it perfectly
  • Praise every attempt, however imperfect
Experienced — step up from the start
  • Jog pace; weak foot preferred on slalom
  • Receive and turn away on Station B (2 touches)
  • Skill moves between sole stops on Station A
  • Help newer teammates understand each drill
💡
Coaching tip: These three drills return every session through Block 1. The only thing that changes is the scaling. Today is about familiarity — players should leave knowing what each station looks like and what it's asking. One clear demonstration per station is worth five minutes of verbal instruction. Celebrate every player who completes the drill, regardless of how it looks.
60 – 70
Knockout 10 min
Season Closing Ritual — runs every session through Dec 12

All 18 line up. Penalty-style — one shot each. Miss = out, score = survive. GK rotates every round. Eliminated players become the crowd and cheer loudly — no one sits in silence. New players may shoot from a closer mark; never draw attention to it. With 10 minutes today, run it all the way to a winner if energy allows. Multiple rounds — every player gets several shots. Run it all the way to a winner.

💡
Coaching tip: Announce Knockout the same way every single session — "Time for Knockout!" — so it becomes the signal that the session is about to end on a high. Consistency builds ritual. Ritual builds belonging.
