0 – 5
Tiki-Tak — Block 2 Closing Opener 5 min
Final Thursday Opening — Block 2

Same Tiki-Tak ritual that has opened every Thursday this block. Groups of 2 or 3, 6 feet apart, one touch, maximum pace. Today coaches join a group each — play with them. Start the session with energy.

5 – 15
Warm-Up: Transition Chase Rondo — Open Rules 10 min

Final rondo of Block 2. Open rules today — no specific progression. Let the players set the pace and the energy. Coaches play alongside if numbers allow.

  • 5v2, same 10×10m square. Players self-organise the swap — no coach needed to call it
  • Defenders win ball → 2v1 → finish any way they want
  • If energy is high and players want to go harder, let them
15 – 45
Block 2 Round-Robin Festival 30 min · 3 rounds of 10 min

19 players → 4 teams (5, 5, 5, 4). Three 10-minute games, rotating opponents each round. Every team plays every other team. Coaches score and announce standings after each round. Full rules, full intensity — this is Block 2's tournament.

Round-Robin Format
  • Round 1 (15–25): Team A vs Team B · Team C vs Team D
  • Round 2 (25–35): Team A vs Team C · Team B vs Team D
  • Round 3 (35–45): Team A vs Team D · Team B vs Team C
  • 1 point per goal, 0 points for a loss. Coach Pete keeps score on both pitches.
  • No conditions — free play, full rules. Best team wins the Block 2 Festival.
  • Coach David announces the standings after each round with full drama.
💡
Coaching tip: Don't coach during the round-robin. Just referee and keep score. The energy of competition is the whole point. If a great moment happens — a perfect press leading to a goal, a clean wall pass, a GK cutting the angle — you can name it at the break between rounds. One sentence, then announce the score.
45 – 60
Block 2 Ceremony — Recognition & Home Challenges 15 min

Bring all 19 players in. Announce the round-robin champion. Then: one specific observation per player — something they did better in Block 2 than in Block 1. No generic praise. Then hand out home challenges.

Structure
  • Announce the champion: Round-robin winner is the Block 2 Festival Champion. Brief moment of celebration.
  • One-by-one recognition (5–6 min): Coach Pete or Coach David names one specific, genuine observation for every player. Not "great work this block" — something specific: "You started scanning before the ball arrived by Week 3. I saw it Tuesday. That's new."
  • Home challenge hand-out (3–4 min): Hand each player their written challenge. Read it to them out loud. Make it feel personal. Examples:
    • New players: juggling record, 50 sole rolls daily, kick against a wall for 10 minutes
    • Experienced: weak-foot juggling record, 1v1 with a sibling, 3 give-and-go sequences in a row
  • Close: "Fall break is Oct 12–16. Block 3 starts Oct 20. Come back ready — Block 3 is where it gets really interesting."
60 – 75
Grand Game — All 19 · Full Pitch 15 min

All 19 on the full pitch in one game. Roughly 9v10. GK optional — use whoever wants it. No conditions, no coaching. Just play. This is Block 2's final full game. Coaches watch from the sideline.

  • No subs — everyone plays the full 15 minutes
  • Full rules. Players referee themselves — coaches only intervene for safety
  • If a player scores, they lead the team chant
75 – 90
⚽ Block 2 Grand Knockout 15 min
Block 2 Closing Ritual — Final Knockout

All 19. GK must step off the line to cut the angle — if they don't, shooter gets a retake. Hustle rule strictly enforced — sprint to ball, sprint back. Panenka and chip attempts fully encouraged. Eliminated players lead crowd chanting. Final Four announced with full ceremony. The Block 2 Knockout Champion is announced by the whole group. They get to assign next session's opening drill to the coaches (juggling or Tiki-Tak).

💡
Closing note: End on a high. The last thing players remember before the break is what they carry into Block 3. Make it loud, fun, and specific. Name the Block 2 Knockout Champion by full name. Shake every player's hand as they leave. See you Oct 20.
