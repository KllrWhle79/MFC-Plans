5 – 15
Warm-Up: Sharks & Minnows 10 min

All 18. 3 sharks try to kick minnows' balls out of the grid. Minnow loses their ball → becomes a shark. Naturally teaches shielding instinct through play — no instruction needed.

  • Grid: 25×25m — everyone has a ball except the 3 sharks
  • Sharks poke or kick minnows' balls out — no body contact
  • Minnow whose ball exits the grid becomes the next shark
  • Progression: sharks must press in pairs — observe whether they naturally trap the minnow
  • Freeze (once only): "What made it easier — going alone or with a partner?"
Sharks & Minnows · 25×25m
💡
Coaching tip: Don't explain shielding before the drill — let them discover it under pressure. After 3–4 minutes you'll see players naturally turning their body to protect the ball. When you see it, name it: "That right there is shielding — your body between the ball and the shark. Remember that." Then move on.

45 – 60
Whiteboard + Water: The Offside Rule 15 min

15 minutes — keep it tight. Three offside scenarios only; draw it, then walk 6 players through it physically on the pitch. Experienced players already know this — use them to demonstrate. Move to shaded area; players sit with water.

The Core Rule — One Sentence (2 min)

"You cannot be past the last defender when your teammate plays the ball to you." Everything else is detail. Land this sentence first before drawing anything.

  • Draw: a defender line, a striker position, the moment of the pass
  • Ask: "Is this player offside?" — let players answer before you explain
  • Key phrase: "It's when the ball is played — not when you receive it."
✓ Onside

Striker is behind or level with the last defender when the ball is played. Keep going.

✗ Offside

Striker is ahead of the last defender when the ball leaves their teammate's foot. Flag up.

= Level = Onside

Level with the last defender = onside. Tie goes to the attacker. This surprises many players.

Offside Rule — Three Scenarios Side-by-Side
On-Pitch Walkthrough (6 min)

Take 6 players onto the pitch. Coach plays the "passer", players walk through all three scenarios physically. Ask the remaining 12: "Is that offside?" for each one. Rotate so more players try the forward role. Finish with one live rep: coach plays the ball, forward runs at the right moment — players call it.

💡
Coaching tip: Experienced players already know this rule — use them. "Can someone who knows the rule come up and show us the offside scenario?" This puts them in a teaching role, reinforces their knowledge, and lets new players learn from a peer. More effective than a coach lecture every time.
60 – 80
Rondo: 4v1 — Week 3 · 20 min 20 min

Only the second rondo session — the format will still feel new for some players. Reintroduce it briefly before starting: "Keep the ball away from the person in the middle." Then go. More time today (20 min vs last session), so shorter rounds and more swaps — everyone gets more turns in the middle.

  • 4 outside players on an 8×8m square, 1 defender in the middle
  • New players: unlimited touches still — focus on the "checking!" call before receiving
  • Experienced players: 2-touch maximum, must shift position after every pass they make
  • Swap defender on interception or after 8 passes
  • Rounds of 2 min with a water sip between — everyone gets multiple turns in the middle today
4v1 Rondo · 8×8m · Week 3
💡
Coaching tip: Week 3 is when you'll start to see the first signs of genuine spatial awareness — a player shuffling sideways to create a better angle without being told. When you see it, stop that player's round for 5 seconds: "Did everyone see what Marcus just did? He moved before the ball arrived. That's the game." Then restart immediately. One teaching moment per rondo block is enough.
80 – 90
Knockout 10 min
Closing Ritual — same every session

All 18 line up. Penalty-style — one shot each. Miss = out, score = survive. GK rotates every round. Eliminated players become the crowd and cheer loudly. New players may shoot from a closer mark; never draw attention to it. 10 minutes — run all the way to a winner today.

💡
Coaching tip: Week 3 is when the ritual becomes real. Players know the format, they know the chant, they've done this twice before. Let them run it. Coaches step back. You announce "Time for Knockout!" — they organise themselves. The more ownership they have, the more powerful the ritual becomes.
