5 – 15
Warm-Up: Physical Contact Games — Lean, Run, Pull 10 min

No balls for the first 7 minutes. Physicality is the focus of this session — the warm-up primes players for body contact before it shows up in the technical block. Three drills, each one step up in contact. Keep the energy high and the tone fun — this isn't a fight, it's skill.

  • Back-to-back lean (2 min): pairs stand back-to-back, arms linked. On "Go!" both push back into each other and hold — 5 seconds. Feel the resistance. Switch to side-to-side lean: shoulder to shoulder, each player trying to hold their ground. Cue: "Low hips, bend your knees. Strength comes from your legs, not your shoulders."
  • Run shoulder-to-shoulder (3 min): pairs run side by side along a 20m line — shoulders touching the whole way. On "Switch!" the outside player bumps the inside player off the line — light bump, stay upright. Inside player resists and holds the line. Switch roles. Progress: slight speed increase each rep. Cue: "Stay low. Your body wins, not your arms."
  • Pull-away / hold-back (2 min): pairs face each other, both grip the same bib or belt loop (no wrists). On "Go!" one player tries to run away, the other tries to hold them back for 3 seconds. Then release and swap. Cue: "Low centre of gravity wins every time." Not about strength — about position.
  • Final 3 min: free dribble — back to balls, shake out the legs, transition into the technical block mindset.
💡
Coaching tip: The boys will love this — but set the tone in the first 30 seconds. "This is a skill session, not a wrestling match. Low hips, legal contact, no shoving. The player who wins is the one with better technique — not the biggest one." Then let them go. If it gets too rough, reset with one sentence and continue. Physical contact in a structured context is exactly how to build comfort with it.

45 – 60
Whiteboard + Water: Corner Kicks & Goal Kicks 15 min

15 minutes. Cover when each restart happens and basic delivery options. Connect to the U10 pitch diagram — the corner arc and goal area are already familiar from the Week 1 field diagram. Then practice 2–3 corners per side on the pitch immediately after.

↗ Corner Kick — when?

Ball goes over the goal line last touched by a defender. Attacker takes the kick from the corner arc on that side. Can score directly.

⬦ Goal Kick — when?

Ball goes over the goal line last touched by an attacker. GK (or any defender) kicks from inside the 6-yard box. Must exit the penalty area before anyone touches it.

Corner Kick Delivery — Two Options Only (5 min)
  • Near-post run: one player attacks the near post — first contact wins. Simple, direct.
  • Far-post arrival: ball played deeper, far-post player arrives late and meets it. Harder to defend.
  • Rule reminder: opponents must be 10 yards from the corner arc when the kick is taken
Corner Kick — Near Post vs Far Post Run
On-Pitch Practice (5 min)

Take all 18 to the pitch immediately after drawing it. Three groups run 2–3 corners each from both sides. One coach delivers, two coaches watch positioning. Ask: "Corner or goal kick?" before each restart to reinforce recognition. End with 2 live corner kicks into a 3v3 scenario in the box.

💡
Coaching tip: The single biggest win from this session is players correctly calling "corner!" or "goal kick!" in the rondo and SSG that follows. When it happens — name it. "Did you hear Marcus? He called it before I did. That's soccer IQ." That's the transfer you're looking for.

Rondo: 4v1 — Week 4 · 20 min 20 min

Week 4 rondo: same 4v1 format, same 8×8m square. The format is now fully owned by the players — coaches should step back further and let it run. Final 5 minutes: fully player-run with no coach input.

  • 4 outside players, 1 defender in the middle, 8×8m square
  • New players: "checking!" call mandatory before every receive — coaches affirm each one
  • Experienced players: 2-touch maximum, must shift position after every pass, name target before playing
  • Swap defender on interception or 8 consecutive passes
  • Rounds of 2–3 min, water sip between
  • 60–75 min: coached rounds
  • 75–80 min: fully player-run — coaches do not speak

Knockout 10 min
Closing Ritual — same every session

All 18. Penalty-style — miss = out. GK rotates every round. Eliminated players cheer loudly. Week 4: let the players set up and organise Knockout themselves — coaches only announce "Time for Knockout!" and then step back. Run all the way to a winner.

💡
Coaching tip: After the final shot, gather all 18 for 60 seconds. One question: "What does a corner kick mean again?" Take quick-fire answers — they'll know. Reinforce: "Watch for it in the rondo on Thursday." Quick close, send them off on a high.
