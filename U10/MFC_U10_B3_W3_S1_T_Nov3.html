Tuesday Opening Ritual — Block 3

Players arrive and juggle freely in their own space — feet only. No targets, no records. Just touch and feel. If someone hits a personal best, they call it out. Coach acknowledges, keeps moving.

5 – 15
Warm-Up: Free-Dribble Mastery — Final Session 10 min · Free Dribble 2 of 2 · No Dribble Station Today

Large grid (~30×30m). Every player has a ball. All three coaches circulate and add individual nudges. The warm-up IS the dribble work today — no dribble station in the station rotation.

Three Variations · 3–4 min each
  • Variation 1 — Free dribble: find space, change speed and direction on your own timing. Goal: no standing still. No two touches in the same direction for more than 5m.
  • Variation 2 — Scissor into switch sides: same as W1 — approach cone, plant foot, perform scissor, use outside of the other foot to push across. Opposite side = success.
  • Variation 3 — Front-foot stop + directional cut: dribble to a cone, use sole of the front foot to trap the ball dead, then take one touch in a completely new direction with the outside of either foot. Pause = 0 seconds (roll straight into the cut).
💡
Coaching tip — Variation 3: The common error is lifting the foot onto the ball too early (while still running). The stop only works at close range. Coach the approach speed: "Slow as you arrive — one touch stop — explode out." Players who stay fast into the ball will fumble the stop.
15 – 60
Technical Stations: Pressing Wave · Full-Sprint Dribbling · Defending Shape 45 min · 15 min per station

No dribble station today — warm-up covered it. Stations are Pressing (Pete), Full-Sprint Dribbling (Brent), and Defending Body Shape (David). All three progress from their Week 1–2 versions.

⟳ ROTATION
Whistle every 15 minutes. Coach stays at station. Groups rotate: Gold → Green → Blue → Gold.
Gold · Coach Pete · Station A — Pressing Patience: Full 3v3 Press Wave

Upgrade from W2's 3-man shape to a full press wave. 3 attackers try to play out — 3 defenders press together. First defender closes the ball, second covers the most dangerous pass, third cuts the escape lane. Rotate attackers and defenders every 2 rounds. Cue: "Press together — don't leave gaps."

Green · Coach Brent · Station B — Full-Sprint Dribbling: Front Defender Variant

Same long-touch gate as previous sessions. New element: a front-facing defender stands 5m past the gate and reacts on the dribbler's first touch through. Dribbler must accelerate past or cut around. Ball wins if they reach the end cone. Defender wins if they get a toe on the ball. Cue: "One big touch — now SPRINT."

Blue · Coach David · Station C — Defending Body Shape: Side-Low-Fast-Slow

1v1 defending. Attacker dribbles from a cone. Defender starts 3m away and must arrive in side-on shape before the attacker reaches them — not after. Mantra aloud: "Side — Low — Fast arrival — Slow show." Three consecutive reps per pair, then switch roles. Cue: "Mantra out loud. Say it while you do it."

60 – 80
SSG: Dog Fight — 3rd Appearance 20 min

Third Dog Fight of Block 3. Players know the format. Coaching focus shifts to transition decisions — which direction to attack the moment possession is won.

Setup Reminder
  • Wide & short field. 3 pug goals each end (one central, two wide). Whole team together. 2 balls in play simultaneously.
  • All restarts apply (throw-ins, goal kicks, corners). GKs count as outfield.
  • Restart immediately when a ball goes dead — the other ball is still live.
Coaching Focus — Transition Direction
  • As soon as your team wins the ball: which goal am I attacking? Pick a target goal before the first pass.
  • If a player immediately turns back after winning possession — stop play and ask: "Which goal are you attacking?"
  • Communication: call out which goal to attack before the ball moves. "Left goal! Left goal!"
  • Reward any team that wins possession and immediately plays forward to a goal — loud recognition from the sideline
⚡
Cue: "Win it — pick a goal — go!" Three-word sequence. Coach calls it from the sideline when a team wins possession and hesitates. After the third time hearing it, the players should call it to each other.
