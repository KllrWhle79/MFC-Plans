Teaching Juggling to First-Timers

Many players will have never juggled before. The goal today is simply a positive first experience with the ball in the air — not a perfect technique. Use these progressions quietly and individually as you circulate, not as a group instruction:

  • Start from the hand: Hold the ball, drop it, let it bounce once, then kick it back up to your hand. One touch at a time. This removes the pressure of keeping a sequence going and builds the feel of a clean contact.
  • Foot shape matters most: Toes up, ankle locked, strike with the laces (top of the foot). If the ball is flying sideways or spinning wildly, the foot is floppy — cue "lock the ankle, toes to the sky."
  • Small touches, not big kicks: The ball should only go a foot or two above the knee. Big kicks = loss of control. Cue: "tap it, don't kick it."
  • Thighs are a superpower: For new players, thigh juggles are easier and more forgiving. Encourage alternating foot → thigh → foot — it slows the sequence down and builds confidence.
  • Eyes on the ball: Remind players to watch the ball all the way onto their foot, not look up between touches. "Watch the ball land on your foot."
  • The drop-and-catch progression: Drop → one touch → catch. Then drop → two touches → catch. Build sequences of one rep at a time. Any player who can't get to 1 yet should work this way for several sessions — it's normal and fine.
💡
Coaching tip: This is the very first moment of the season. The energy you bring right now sets the tone for everything. Be enthusiastic, call out names, count reps loudly. Never compare players to each other. "That's your best yet!" is the only coaching phrase needed here. Make them feel something great is starting.

Session 1 — The Soccer Field

Draw the field — label touchlines, goal lines, centre circle, penalty area, build-out line. Explain when each restart happens. Keep it to 3–4 concepts max.

  • Ball over touchline → throw-in for the other team
  • Ball over goal line off a defender → corner kick for the attacking team
  • Ball over goal line off an attacker → goal kick for the defending team
  • Hands: only the goalkeeper, only inside the penalty area

Rondo Introduction: 4v1 25 min

First rondo of the season. Many of these players have never played one. Introduce it in one sentence and let them figure it out. 25 minutes gives everyone plenty of turns — short rounds of 2–3 min with water sips between. Everyone gets multiple turns in the middle.

  • Groups of 5: 4 outside players on corners of an 8×8m square, 1 defender in the middle
  • Introduce it in one sentence: "Keep the ball away from the person in the middle." Then start it.
  • Unlimited touches for everyone today
  • Swap defender on interception or after 8 consecutive passes
  • Water sip between rounds — rounds of 2–3 min so everyone gets turns in the middle
4v1 Rondo · 8×8m
💡
Coaching tip: Introduce it in one sentence: "Keep the ball away from the person in the middle." Then start it. Don't explain passing angles, movement, or spacing — let them discover it. After a couple of minutes you'll naturally see players calling for the ball and shifting to create space. That instinct is exactly what rondos build. Celebrate every string of 5+ passes loudly.

Knockout — First of the Season 10 min
Season Closing Ritual — runs every session through Dec 12

All 18 line up. Penalty-style — one shot each. Miss = out, score = survive. GK rotates every round. Eliminated players become the crowd and cheer loudly — no one sits in silence. New players may shoot from a closer mark; never draw attention to it. Run as many rounds as time allows — everyone gets several shots. The extra time lets the ritual breathe and feel important from day one.

💡
Coaching tip: Announce Knockout the same way every single session — "Time for Knockout!" — so it becomes the signal that the session is about to end on a high. Consistency builds ritual. Ritual builds belonging.
