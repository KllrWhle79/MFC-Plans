0 – 5
Juggling — Block 1 Final Records 5 min
Opening Ritual — same every session through Dec 12

All 18. This is the final Block 1 juggling session. Every player tries for their all-time personal best. Any record broken today gets announced to the group. Coaches count alongside — be present and loud. The ritual continues in Block 2; this is just the end of its first chapter.

💡
Coaching tip: Write down every player's final Block 1 juggling number before the session ends. It's the baseline for Block 2 — and a metric you can use to celebrate specific growth at the start of next block. "Jackson, your Block 1 record was 11. Let's see where Block 2 takes it."
5 – 15
Warm-Up: Street Football — No Coaching 10 min

All 18, free play, no structure, no coaching, no instruction. Players choose their game. All three coaches sit on the sideline and watch. This is an intentional gift — and a diagnostic. What they do without being told is what they've actually internalised.

💡
Coaching tip: Resist everything. The temptation to organise, to suggest, to point out something — ignore it for 10 minutes. Watch for: players calling "checking!" on their own; players enforcing throw-in rules without a referee; players using the zone vocabulary ("I'm the winger!"). Every one of those is a Block 1 win. Write them down — they become Thursday's closing compliments if you haven't already sourced them.
15 – 45
Technical Stations: Block 1 Final Run — Same Drills, Positive Reps Only 30 min · 10 min per station

Same three stations for the last time in Block 1. No new concepts, no new pressure. The goal is confidence and enjoyment — coaches give only positive reinforcement this block. Players should leave feeling proud of what they've built over 9 sessions.

Gold · Coach Pete · Station A — Shield + Play Out

Ball mastery + shield. All players: comfortable reps, coaches celebrate every clean moment. Exp: skill move to turn out if they want — no pressure.

Blue · Coach Brent · Station B — Receive, Turn, Pass

Paired first touch + turn. Same as all week. "Checking!" call, receive cleanly, turn. If a player lands something they struggled with in Week 3 — name it for the group.

White · Coach David · Station C — Full Circuit

1v1 defending channel. Same as all week. Hold shape, show outside, step in or tackle on heavy touch. Let players call their own level — no coach pressure today.

ROTATION
Whistle every 10 minutes. Keep the energy high — this is a celebration of Block 1 work, not a test. Coaches: positive reinforcement only in this block today.
💡
Coaching tip: The technical block is where your written compliments get confirmed or updated. A player who has been quiet all Block suddenly lands a perfect 1-touch turn? That's the compliment. Be present in a way you can't always afford to be when you're also coaching technique.
45 – 60
Final Whiteboard: Players Quiz the Coaches 15 min

The last whiteboard session of the entire season. Flip the format — players ask questions or answer challenges. Then coaches announce the transition to Block 2 (play-based, no more whiteboards). Close with the tournament talk for the Gold team.

Players Quiz the Coaches — Block 1 Rapid Fire (7 min)

Coach Pete runs it. One rule: if a player can answer correctly before the coach finishes explaining, they score a point for their group. Keep it moving — one question every 30–40 seconds. Use these questions or generate your own:

  • "What's a foul throw — show me the three rules?" (any player demonstrates)
  • "The striker doesn't have the ball — where do they go?" (drops to halfway)
  • "Defender is under pressure from a winger — what can our defender do?" (push up for backpass)
  • "Corner kick — the ball went over the goal line off a defender. Who takes it?" (attacker)
  • "GK picks up the ball — how long do they have?" (6 seconds)
  • "What does 'checking!' mean and when do you say it?" (before receiving)
  • "The CM — what do we call them and why?" (The Glue — connects defence to attack)
Block 2 Transition Announcement (3 min)

"From the next session — no more whiteboards. Everything we do in Block 2 is through the game. That's how the best players in the world learn — not from lectures, but from playing. You've built the foundation. Block 2 is where you start using it."

Leave space for questions. Players may be curious or even anxious about the change. Reassure: "You'll still learn — you'll just learn by playing. That's the best way."

💡
Coaching tip: When a new player answers a question correctly — especially a technical one — make the most of it. "Jackson, six weeks ago you didn't know what a throw-in was. Now you just explained the offside rule." That specific contrast is worth ten generic "great job" moments.
60 – 80
6v6 Full Match — Block 1 Grand Game 20 min

All 18 players in one game — two teams of 9, playing 6v6 with 3 rotating out. Coaches referee properly. All rules enforced. Players call their own restarts. This is the closest to a real match of Block 1 — coaches observe, almost never stop play.

  • Format: 9v9 squad, 6v6 on pitch at a time, 3 subs per team rotating every 3 minutes
  • All rules: throw-ins, corners, goal kicks, free kicks, offside, penalties
  • Players call restarts before coaches — coaches only intervene if wrong
  • GKs rotate every 5 minutes — all players experience GK at some point
  • Coaches do not stop play to coach — one sentence maximum if needed, then play resumes
  • Observe: who holds their positional zone, who calls "checking!", who defends back to the build-out line as a winger, who drops as striker when we don't have the ball
  • Score doesn't matter — coaches don't keep score. Players can if they want.

💡
Coaching tip: This is a diagnostic game as much as it is a celebration. Take mental notes on who holds their position naturally, who presses intelligently, who communicates. These observations inform your Block 2 positional decisions. You don't need to say anything during the game — just watch. The game is the teacher now.

Block 1 Grand Knockout · Closing Ceremony · Tournament Send-Off 10 min
Grand Knockout — Block 1 Final

All 18. Standard penalty Knockout. Coach Pete announces each player's name as they step up — full name, no rushing. GK uses angle play (set the stance, step off the line). Eliminated players lead the crowd chant. Run to a winner. 5 minutes maximum.

Winner gets 5 seconds of pure crowd noise from all 18. Then gather everyone.

Pre-Tournament Talk — OSI Champions Cup · Flagstaff · Sep 11–13
  • What to expect: multiple games across the weekend, potentially against teams you've never seen. That's the fun — you don't know what's coming, and neither do they.
  • Represent the club: MFC stands for something. Win or lose, the way you play and carry yourselves matters more than the result. Be the team other coaches point to.
  • Use what you know: "Checking!" before you receive. Striker drops to halfway when we don't have the ball. Wingers stay wide and track back. GK steps off the line. You've done this for 5 weeks. It's yours.
  • Hotels and travel: rest when you can, eat well, stay together as a team off the pitch. Tournaments are won in the evenings as much as on the field.
  • Results: coaches care about effort and identity. A 3–0 loss where you played our way is better than a 3–0 win where you didn't. Play MFC soccer.
  • Have fun: this is why you play. Flagstaff in September is beautiful. Enjoy every minute of it.
💡
Coaching tip: The closing ceremony only works if the compliments are specific and prepared. Generic praise is instantly forgotten. A specific, observed compliment — especially one that connects to growth over the whole block — will be remembered for the season. Write them tonight if you haven't already.
